# Marketing / Growth lens

Use this lens for bounded decisions about audience, positioning, message, offer presentation, channel, acquisition, activation, retention, referral, conversion, or growth experiments. Apply the canonical reality protocol first.

## Routing boundary

- Select this as primary when the central decision is how a defined audience should discover, understand, choose, adopt, retain, or advocate for an offer.
- Use business/venture for the commercial model and project/product for the delivered experience. Add one only when it changes the growth decision.
- A request to “do marketing” is not yet a strategy. Bound the audience, behaviour, offer, channel, horizon, and decision.
- Keep legal, regulated financial, medical, clinical, personal-relationship, and family advice outside this lens; require authoritative review for material advertising, privacy, or platform-rule uncertainty.

## Supported decisions and outcomes

- Choose a target audience, category or frame, positioning, message, proof, offer, channel, sequence, budget stage, and experiment.
- Diagnose the binding growth constraint across awareness, relevance, credibility, access, conversion, first value, retention, expansion, and referral.
- Decide whether to test, scale, narrow, reposition, change channel, improve the offer or experience, or stop.
- Optimise for incremental customer behaviour and viable economics, not activity volume.

## Decision-relevant evidence

- Audience evidence: observed needs, contexts, alternatives, eligibility, reachability, willingness to act, and heterogeneity across cohorts.
- Behaviour across the full path: qualified reach, attention, response, conversion, activation, realised value, retention, expansion, referral, and churn.
- Incrementality evidence from a credible counterfactual, holdout, time-series break, matched comparison, or other design proportionate to the decision.
- Economics: incremental contribution, acquisition and fulfilment cost, payback, saturation, cannibalisation, returns, discounting, and channel dependence.
- Message and positioning evidence from comprehension, recall, perceived relevance, credibility, objection, and behaviour—not internal preference.
- Channel evidence: audience fit, auction or distribution dynamics, delivery reliability, measurement limits, platform dependence, and creative fatigue.

## Causal mechanisms

- Trace the mechanism from audience context through attention, interpretation, trust, action, first value, repeat value, and economics.
- Distinguish correlation and platform attribution from incremental effect. Attribution assigns credit under a model; it does not by itself establish causality.
- Diagnose the bottleneck before changing spend or creative. More traffic does not repair a weak offer, inaccessible product, poor first value, or retention failure.
- Treat audience, message, offer, channel, and experience as separable hypotheses where possible; vary one meaningful factor or use a design that can identify the mechanism.
- Expect saturation, selection effects, novelty decay, competitive response, and channel feedback.

## Stakeholder agency

- Treat prospects and customers as autonomous people who can ignore, compare, distrust, delay, opt out, switch, complain, or leave.
- Do not infer motives or vulnerability from segment labels. Use observed context and behaviour, and preserve consent and refusal.
- Treat platforms, partners, creators, competitors, and sales teams as adaptive actors with incentives and changing rules.
- Do not design around hidden manipulation, material omission, fabricated urgency, dark patterns, or unauthorised targeting.

## Characteristic failure modes

- Vanity-metric substitution: treating impressions, clicks, followers, leads, or attributed conversions as the business outcome.
- Attribution certainty: crediting the last touch or platform report without a defensible counterfactual.
- Audience averaging: hiding materially different needs, economics, or harm behind blended performance.
- Message-first diagnosis: rewriting copy when offer, proof, access, price, first value, or retention is the constraint.
- Premature scale: increasing budget on a selected cohort, temporary novelty, weak measurement, or deteriorating marginal economics.
- Channel dependency: allowing one platform's rules, auction, or measurement model to become an unexamined business constraint.
- Growth at any cost: ignoring contribution, payback, service load, churn, cannibalisation, trust, or brand damage.

## Out-of-scope boundaries

- Do not provide jurisdiction-specific advertising, privacy, consumer, platform, medical, financial-promotion, or intellectual-property conclusions.
- Do not invent audience size, benchmark rates, attribution, incrementality, costs, conversion, or revenue.
- Do not facilitate deceptive claims, fabricated proof, covert profiling, discriminatory exclusion, dark patterns, spam, or unauthorised personal-data use.
- Do not call a campaign commercially validated when only creative delivery or top-of-funnel activity is evidenced.

## Readiness implications

- **Ready:** the audience, desired behaviour, offer, mechanism, channel, measurement, economics, and downside controls adequately support the bounded action.
- **Conditional:** a staged test is justified only with named spend, audience, measurement, consent, economics, saturation, or stop conditions.
- **Not validated:** the audience, positioning, mechanism, incremental effect, economics, or retention link lacks discriminating evidence. Run a bounded experiment or repair the upstream constraint.
- **Infeasible as posed:** a demonstrated access, policy, economics, trust, measurement, or offer constraint blocks the exact channel or growth plan. Missing evidence alone is not infeasibility.

## Application checklist

1. Define the audience, desired behaviour, offer, channel, horizon, and economic outcome.
2. Locate the binding constraint across awareness, relevance, trust, conversion, first value, retention, and economics.
3. Separate attributed activity from incremental effect and label measurement limits.
4. Design the smallest audience-message-offer-channel test that can discriminate the live hypotheses.
5. Set success, failure, spend, consent, saturation, stop, and review conditions.
