# People leadership lens

Use this lens for decisions about team or individual outcomes, role clarity, capability, environment, incentives, feedback, support, accountability, role design, or lawful and policy-consistent performance consequences. Apply the canonical reality protocol first and preserve each person's agency.

## Routing boundary

- Select this as the primary lens when the central decision concerns leading a team or managing an individual's work outcomes, development, performance, role, or accountability.
- Select it as a secondary lens only when another supported professional decision is primary and team capability, operating conditions, or leadership consequences materially change the evidence, risk, or action.
- Do not route a manager's own advancement question here unless a distinct team decision is material; use the career lens for the personal career outcome.
- Personal, intimate, family, household, and whole-life circumstances may shape a leadership decision, but this lens governs role-based leadership mechanisms only. Apply the core directly to the wider circumstances.

## Supported decisions and outcomes

- Define the required work outcome, quality, behaviour, role boundary, time horizon, and evidence of the current gap.
- Decide how to clarify expectations, change the system, provide resources, coach or train, redesign work, align incentives, give feedback, create accountability, reassign responsibility, or apply proportionate formal consequences.
- Distinguish capability, capacity, clarity, process, dependency, incentive, support, conduct, and commitment hypotheses before choosing an intervention.
- Improve team reliability and learning without pretending every person's preferences can or should be controlled.
- Set observable milestones, support commitments, decision points, consequences, and contingencies for refusal, non-improvement, or exit.

## Decision-relevant evidence

- The explicit role, expected outcome, quality bar, priority, deadline, authority, and whether those expectations were understood and stable.
- Observable work outputs, outcomes, incidents, decisions, follow-through, feedback history, and patterns across time and contexts—not personality labels.
- Work conditions: staffing, workload, tools, process, dependencies, interruptions, incentives, psychological and physical safety, and conflicting priorities.
- Capability evidence from demonstrated tasks, relevant practice, learning trajectory, and support already provided; distinguish lack of opportunity from lack of skill.
- The employee's account, manager's account, peer or customer evidence, and system data, each labelled by provenance and possible selection effects.
- Applicable policy, role authority, documented commitments, available support, alternative staffing or role designs, and the costs of delay or turnover.

## Causal mechanisms

- Compare at least the live rival explanations: unclear expectations, insufficient capability, insufficient capacity, broken environment or process, misaligned incentives, unresolved conflict, low commitment, or deliberate refusal.
- Link each intervention to the mechanism it could change. Training does not fix a conflicting priority; pressure does not fix missing access; support does not substitute indefinitely for accountability.
- Use feedback to make the gap and consequence legible, support to make success feasible, and accountability to make commitments and trade-offs real.
- Test system-level explanations before attributing a recurring result solely to the individual, while retaining the possibility of individual responsibility when evidence supports it.
- Model team feedback effects: incentives, exceptions, workload redistribution, perceived fairness, trust, and precedent can change other people's behaviour.

## Stakeholder agency

- Treat employees and peers as autonomous actors who can explain, disagree, refuse, adapt, improve, seek transfer, escalate, or leave.
- Do not diagnose a disorder, personality, attitude, loyalty, or motive from work outcomes. Keep these claims reported, inferred, assumption, or unknown unless directly supported within the relevant professional decision.
- Build plans that remain coherent if the person does not agree or improve. Include a legitimate escalation, role, staffing, or exit contingency rather than assuming compliance.
- Use incentives, feedback, support, and accountability openly within role authority. Do not treat asymmetric power as proof that a response is voluntary or predictable.

## Characteristic failure modes

- Character substitution: replacing an observable performance gap and causal analysis with lazy, toxic, disloyal, difficult, or high-potential labels.
- Single-cause certainty: selecting attitude, skill, workload, or process before comparing plausible rivals.
- Expectation hindsight: holding someone to an unstated, unstable, or conflicting standard.
- Support-only drift: repeatedly adding coaching or accommodation without a decision point, ownership, or consequence.
- Accountability-only pressure: escalating stakes while capability, access, safety, priorities, or system defects remain unresolved.
- Deterministic optimisation: designing incentives or pressure as if a person can be engineered into unquestioning compliance.
- Hero-manager bias: assuming the leader's account is complete, neutral, or the only authoritative perspective.
- Team externalities: solving one person's gap by silently overloading reliable staff or degrading fairness and retention.

## Out-of-scope boundaries

- Do not diagnose medical, psychological, neurodevelopmental, or personality conditions; do not infer protected or private characteristics.
- Do not provide jurisdiction-specific employment, legal, clinical, or safety conclusions without authoritative current evidence.
- Do not facilitate material deception, coercion, exploitation, retaliation, credential misuse, or hidden monitoring. Name the conduct and use legitimate evidence, authority, and process.
- Do not apply managerial optimisation to intimate, family, or household relationships. Analyse their distinct consent, care, trust, dependency, and autonomy conditions directly.
- Do not claim a performance outcome proves character, motive, future compliance, or general worth.

## Readiness implications

- **Ready:** the expected outcome and authority are clear; the performance evidence is current and proportionate; live causal explanations have been compared; support and accountability fit the evidence; and refusal or non-improvement has a legitimate contingency.
- **Conditional:** a support plan or accountability step is justified if named expectations, resources, policy checks, milestones, or review conditions are established first.
- **Not validated:** the gap, standard, cause, authority, or likely response rests mainly on labels, one account, or ideal compliance. Clarify expectations, collect proportionate evidence, test a rival, or use a reversible intervention.
- **Infeasible as posed:** a demonstrated authority, policy, safety, capability, resource, or role constraint blocks the exact plan. Identify role redesign, staffing, sequence, or escalation alternatives; missing evidence alone does not establish the block.

## Application checklist

1. State the required outcome, evidence of the gap, role authority, horizon, and consequence of delay.
2. Separate observations and reports from character or motive inferences.
3. Compare clarity, capability, capacity, environment, incentive, support, conduct, and commitment explanations.
4. Match support and accountability to the strongest current mechanism, including effects on the wider team.
5. Define milestones, review point, and a legitimate contingency for disagreement, refusal, non-improvement, transfer, or exit.
