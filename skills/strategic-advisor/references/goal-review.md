# Material goal review

Use this contract only after the underlying outcome and goal purpose have been
qualified under [goal-qualification.md](goal-qualification.md). It distinguishes
material progress from activity, detects drift, challenges goal changes, and
records separate decisions about the goal and the path. It is generic public
reasoning, not a public store of a person's private strategy.

## Route the review

Cadence guarantees coverage; it must not delay a material decision.

- **Event review** pre-empts the schedule when new outcome evidence, a changed
  constraint, a causal-path falsifier, a stop condition, a proposed goal
  amendment, a material offline outcome, or an owner correction could change
  the supported action. Inspect the trigger, affected claims and commitments,
  immediate containment or reconciliation need, and the smallest safe decision.
- **Weekly pulse** is a configurable execution review. Reconcile the evidence
  window, material progress, work that consumed effort without advancing the
  goal, new constraints or drift, and only the next commitments justified by
  current strategy. Do not reopen the goal merely because execution was hard.
- **Monthly portfolio review** is a configurable goal-and-allocation review.
  Reassess value, horizon, causal path, falsifiers, evidence quality, portfolio
  role, allocation, opportunity cost, offline outcomes, constraints, strongest
  rival, and separate goal/path dispositions.

Weekly and monthly are operating conventions, not universal laws. Configure,
combine, reduce, or replace their intervals when decision tempo and review cost
support another design. Never turn cadence into an accountability ritual.

## Require decision-relevant evidence

Start from the current owner-settled outcome, unacceptable substitutes, object
class, goal purpose, exact-action readiness, causal bridge, constraints,
falsifiers, previous dispositions, and affected commitments. Set an evidence
cut-off and preserve provenance.

For each material item, record whether it is an observation, attributed report,
inference, assumption, unknown, preference, or forecast. An offline outcome may
be recorded as a dated owner report with scope, freshness, limitations, and any
contradiction. Absence from an execution system is not evidence that the
outcome did not occur. If an owner report conflicts with another source,
preserve both claims and apply the contradiction protocol before relying on
either.

Ask the owner only for owner-only outcomes, values, constraints, authority,
acceptable trade-offs, corrections, or offline results whose answer can change
the supported action. Research accessible empirical forks first. Do not repeat
a settled question when all live answers support the same robust move.

## Apply the material-progress test

Work materially advanced the goal only when current evidence supports movement
in at least one of these categories:

1. **Outcome movement** — the underlying outcome or a valid lag result changed.
2. **Driver movement** — an evidenced predictor or controllable causal driver
   moved in the expected direction within its stated lag and limitations.
3. **Constraint movement** — a binding constraint was measurably relaxed,
   removed, exposed, or made more tractable.
4. **Decision movement** — a material uncertainty was resolved or a decision
   was unlocked, made, stopped, or narrowed enough to change supported action.

Activity, effort, task completion, output count, shipping, praise, visibility,
and time spent are execution evidence, not automatic progress. Record them as
**non-advancing effort/output** unless a named causal contribution passes the
test above.

### Credit an enabling project or tool proportionately

Keep an enabler separate from the goal it serves. Record:

- the exact outcome, driver, constraint, or decision it was intended to move;
- why it was necessary rather than merely convenient;
- the evidence that the expected contribution occurred;
- the contribution limit and lag; and
- the falsifier or expiry condition.

Use **enabled but not yet advanced** only when the tool or project created
capacity against a named outcome, driver, constraint, or decision. State that
exact causal boundary; broad `enabled` wording must not imply progress beyond
it. If even that bounded contribution is unsupported, record only the shipped
output and the unknown contribution. Completion of the enabler never proves
completion or progress of the goal.

## Test drift before accepting change

Compare current work, measures, allocation, and proposed decisions with the
latest owner-settled outcome, unacceptable substitutes, horizon, constraints,
causal bridge, falsifiers, and portfolio role.

Goal or path drift exists when, without an explicit supported decision:

- a means, proxy, metric, project, tool, task, or output replaces the outcome;
- the outcome, horizon, success condition, constraint, or acceptable trade-off
  materially changes;
- work or allocation persists after its causal bridge or stop condition fails;
- a path change is presented as proof that the goal changed; or
- a new attractive option silently displaces the approved goal or strongest
  live rival.

A bounded tactic change inside the same supported causal path is not goal
drift. Neither execution friction, non-execution, boredom, novelty, sunk cost,
preference repetition, nor failure of one mechanism proves that the goal should
change.

### Apply the amendment burden of proof

A proposed goal amendment must name:

1. the current goal version and exact proposed replacement or revision;
2. the qualifying evidence, constraint, owner-value, trade-off, authority, or
   opportunity-cost delta;
3. why the current goal or a smaller path correction no longer remains the
   strongest supported option;
4. effects on commitments, evidence validity, portfolio allocation, and
   unacceptable substitutes; and
5. explicit owner authority for any owner-only outcome or value change.

Challenge an unsupported amendment and preserve the last supported goal. Permit
revision when evidence shows that the goal is obsolete, based on a mistaken
premise, infeasible at the relevant horizon/resources, no longer worth its
trade-offs, or dominated by a better evidenced opportunity—and the owner
settles any value-dependent choice. Prior approval does not make a goal
immutable.

## Record two separate dispositions

Record a **goal disposition** and a **path disposition** separately. Each uses
one of the following values and names its exact target:

- **Continue** — retain the current target and basis.
- **Correct** — repair wording, measure, evidence, execution, or a bounded path
  defect without changing the underlying outcome.
- **Pivot** — materially revise the goal or causal strategy under a qualifying
  delta and owner authority where required.
- **Pause** — suspend commitment pending a named condition or decision.
- **Replace** — substitute the exact goal or path with a named supported rival.
- **Stop** — end the exact goal or path and close its affected commitments.

One axis may change while the other remains stable: for example, continue the
goal while replacing the tool, or stop a goal while closing its path. Never use
one combined status that hides which object changed.

## Portable review record

Retain these fields for every material review:

| Field | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Review identity | Stable ID, event/weekly/monthly type, date, evidence window and cut-off |
| Authority | Goal owner, reviewer, decision authority and owner-settled state version |
| Goal state | Underlying outcome, unacceptable substitutes, goal purpose, horizon, constraints and portfolio role |
| Path state | Exact candidate/path, readiness, causal bridge, indicators, lag, falsifiers and stop conditions |
| Evidence | Provenance-linked outcome, driver, constraint and decision movement; offline owner reports; contradictions and limitations |
| Non-progress | Effort, outputs and enablers that did not pass the material-progress test |
| Drift/amendment | Drift verdict, proposed change, qualifying delta, strongest retain-current rival and burden-of-proof result |
| Goal decision | Goal target, one disposition, reason and owner authority where required |
| Path decision | Path target, one disposition, reason and owner authority where required |
| Follow-through | Affected commitments, selected next moves, downstream commitment outputs and unresolved owner reconciliation |
| Reversal and review | Reversal or reopening trigger, next event/date and evidence required |
| Closeout | Closed target, residual obligations, stopped or transferred commitments, final evidence boundary and record status |

A consumer may receive selected commitments or execution evidence, but it does
not become the strategic authority. Durable personal records belong only in an
owner-authorised private Strategy Workspace. Keep public source, examples,
tests, packages, and evaluation material synthetic or irreversibly sanitised.

## Falsify the review process itself

Reduce, combine, correct, or stop the scheduled process when proportionate
evidence shows that it is wasting time. Material signals include:

- repeated reviews cannot change a decision, commitment, evidence need, or
  owner reconciliation and no coverage risk justifies them;
- preparation and discussion cost exceeds the decisions or risk avoided;
- the same settled questions are repeatedly asked despite no action-changing
  delta;
- reports remain dominated by activity or output because required outcome and
  driver evidence is unavailable or unused;
- scheduled review delays an event-triggered correction, pause, replacement,
  or stop;
- records are stale, duplicated, contradictory without resolution, or unused
  by any decision; or
- selected commitments are repeatedly emitted without connection to the
  current strategy.

Correct the evidence design or scope before adding more review. Combine or
reduce scheduled reviews when coverage remains adequate. Stop a scheduled
review when its expected decision value remains below its cost, while retaining
the event exception gate. Fewer questions or shorter meetings are not success
if material drift or owner reconciliation is missed.
