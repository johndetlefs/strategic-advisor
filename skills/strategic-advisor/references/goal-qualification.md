# Goal qualification and causal path

Use this contract when a user presents an ambition, target, metric, mechanism,
project, tool, or task as a goal. Its purpose is to identify a plausible object
for execution without discarding valuable aspirations or laundering an
unsupported route into readiness. It extends the material decision state in
`recommendation-delta.md`; it is not a second goal store.

## Move up before moving down

1. Preserve the stated goal verbatim and its provenance.
2. Move up to the changed external or behavioural condition the user wants,
   why it matters, the horizon, and unacceptable substitutes. A mechanism is
   not the end merely because the user called it the goal.
3. Classify each material object and compare genuinely different routes to the
   underlying outcome. Include a route outside the proposed mechanism when it
   could satisfy the outcome with a different causal chain.
4. Move down through the minimum plausible causal chain for each live route:
   necessary links, assumptions, constraints, lags, evidence, falsifiers, and
   the next decisive validation.
5. Admit only the exact goal or learning commitment supported for its purpose.
   Apply readiness separately to the exact candidate action.

Do not expand the ladder mechanically when the request is routine or the
underlying outcome and route are already sufficiently settled. Use the minimum
altitude and detail that can expose a material proxy or unsupported bridge.

## Classify the stated object

- **End** — the intended changed condition in external or behavioural reality.
- **Means** — a capability or route expected to help cause the end.
- **Proxy** — an indirect quantity or condition treated as success without an
  established enough relationship to the end.
- **Metric** — a measure used to observe an end, driver, constraint, or risk.
- **Project** — a bounded body of work intended to create a result.
- **Tool** — an enabling artifact or system used by a route.
- **Task** — an action or output within a route or project.

These classes describe the object, not its value or readiness. One statement
can contain several objects; classify the material ones separately. A project,
tool, or task earns strategic credit only for the named constraint or causal
link it demonstrably advances.

## Classify goal purpose separately

- **Aspiration** — a desired future condition whose meaning or plausible route
  is not yet developed enough for execution. Preserve it visibly, but keep it
  outside execution commitments until a bounded validation path exists.
- **Discovery goal** — a bounded learning commitment used when an important
  aspiration or route is plausible but causality is unresolved. It must state
  the learning outcome, horizon, falsifier, and decision the result will
  unlock. The discovery action may be executable while the underlying venture,
  rollout, or outcome remains Not validated.
- **Operational goal** — an outcome with a horizon, measure, constraints, a
  causal path supported proportionately to the commitment, usable lag and
  leading indicators, and falsifiers. It may still have a proposed mechanism
  that is Not validated, Conditional, or Infeasible as posed.

Goal purpose never replaces the four readiness states in `readiness.md`.
`Infeasible as posed` applies only to an exact candidate action under its
stated horizon, scope, authority, resources, and constraints; it is not a
fourth goal-purpose class and does not invalidate the underlying goal by
itself.

## Require a plausible causal path

For an operational commitment, identify:

- the outcome and baseline or current state;
- the candidate route and alternative routes considered;
- necessary causal links and the evidence status of each;
- binding constraints, delay, substitution, and failure modes;
- lag measures that observe the outcome;
- leading indicators that are evidenced predictors or controllable drivers;
- falsifiers and stop or revision conditions; and
- the cheapest decisive validation for the weakest load-bearing link.

Plausible means supported enough for the consequence and reversibility of the
next commitment, not certain. When no authoritative route can yet be supported,
retain the aspiration and formulate a discovery goal if a bounded test can
make the route decisionable. Otherwise classify the proposal as a wish or hope
outside execution rather than inventing a plan.

## Prevent indicator and milestone laundering

An initiative, meeting, email, page, build, repeated task, or output count is
not a leading indicator merely because it is early or controllable. For each
candidate indicator, state:

- whether it is a predictor, causal driver, constraint measure, or activity;
- the evidence linking it to the lag outcome;
- its controllability and material confounders;
- the expected lag; and
- what observation would falsify its usefulness.

Treat correlation as prediction unless evidence supports causality. Derive
intermediate lag milestones from the driver model, capacity, timing, and
available evidence. Evenly dividing a final target across weeks, quarters, or
years is arithmetic, not a causal plan.
