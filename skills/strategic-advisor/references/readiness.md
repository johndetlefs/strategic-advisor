# Readiness gate

Assess a proposed strategic direction or candidate action as currently scoped. Use exactly one internal readiness state for the exact action before execution advice or commitment: **Ready**, **Conditional**, **Not validated**, or **Infeasible as posed**. These states calibrate the action; they are not mandatory public labels or a concluding stamp. Explain what can happen next, under what constraints and why. Use a label only when requested or when it makes a consequential distinction clearer. If open exploration has not produced a candidate direction, do not manufacture one merely to assign a verdict.

## Determine the verdict

First identify the decision-critical causal bridge, the strongest material contradiction, the likely binding constraint, and the consequence and reversibility of being wrong. Then use the narrowest applicable state:

- **Ready**: The critical claims are supported enough for the proposed action's consequence and reversibility; no unresolved contradiction is likely to overturn it; and the required authority, capacity, and constraints are adequately understood. Ready means justified to proceed with controls, not certain to succeed.
- **Conditional**: Proceeding is justified only through explicit conditions, limits, sequencing, or a reversible stage. Name every gating condition and how it will be checked. Use this only when the exposure can be bounded responsibly; “conditional” is not a euphemism for an unsupported full commitment.
- **Not validated**: A decision-critical claim, causal bridge, outcome, or constraint lacks adequate support, so execution should pause or become validation work. State the missing validation and the cheapest useful test. Not validated means “not yet supported,” not false or impossible.
- **Infeasible as posed**: An identified, sufficiently established constraint directly conflicts with the objective or plan within the stated scope, horizon, authority, or resources. Name the constraint, its provenance, the conflict, and what would have to change. Missing evidence alone never establishes infeasibility.

When the user is asking for a decision but the candidate action is implicit, formulate the smallest faithful candidate direction and state it before applying readiness. When the user is deliberately exploring and no commitment is pending, remain in exploration and identify the next discriminating question or observation instead.

## Turn the assessment into continuation

Different states must change what the advisor does, not just a heading:

- Supported action: recommend the bounded move and material controls.
- Conditional action: identify the exact permitted stage and observable condition for going further. Do not present the full commitment as approved.
- Missing support: identify the unresolved claim and continue with the cheapest discriminating investigation. Do authorised accessible investigation now; if only the owner can resolve a preference, access or authority gap, ask the focused question and explain what it changes.
- Established conflict: explain what must change, whether an alternative is worth exploring, or why stopping is supported. Do not manufacture research to avoid an evidenced conclusion.

For an unresolved next step, name its owner, the observation or answer sought, and how that returns to the decision. This does not create permission for external actions or a promise to monitor later. Separate support for a test from support for the underlying commitment; a justified test does not validate rollout. Do not end with a status label and leave the user to invent the process.

## Prevent state laundering

- Do not call a case Ready merely because action is urgent, familiar, desired, or endorsed by authority.
- Do not call a case Conditional without enforceable conditions that bound exposure or resolve uncertainty.
- Do not call a case Not validated Infeasible merely because evidence is weak, absent, or difficult to obtain.
- Do not leave a proven constraint conflict as merely Not validated to preserve an attractive narrative.
- Reassess when material evidence, scope, constraints, or the proposed action changes, or a specific prior reasoning error is demonstrated; state what caused the change. Repeated requests or tone alone do not justify it.
