# Organisational influence lens

Use this lens to shape a professional decision through truthful framing, sequencing, negotiation, coalition building, incentive alignment, accountability, and private preparation. Apply the canonical reality protocol first; do not confuse professional influence with deterministic control or with the separate personal-relationship domain.

## Routing boundary

- Select this as the primary lens when the central decision is how to influence a role-based organisational choice, allocation, commitment, policy, priority, or coalition.
- Select it as a secondary lens only when another supported professional outcome is primary and power, decision rights, vetoes, or stakeholder adaptation materially changes the evidence, risk, or action.
- Ordinary professional persuasion, asymmetrical power, private strategy, incentives, negotiation, and accountability remain legitimate subject matter. Their presence does not activate a personal-relationship boundary.
- Personal, intimate, family, and household influence is outside v0 and activates zero lenses unless a separable professional or commercial sub-decision is explicitly bounded. Route business-model or marketing decisions to their own lenses; general-life strategy remains unsupported.

## Supported decisions and outcomes

- Map who can decide, approve, veto, delay, implement, fund, legitimise, or reverse the outcome, including gaps between formal authority and practical power.
- Choose truthful framing, messenger, sequence, forum, coalition, negotiation position, incentive alignment, accountability mechanism, and private preparation.
- Compare stakeholder alternatives and likely adaptive responses; prepare for resistance, renegotiation, delay, partial compliance, coalition shifts, and exit.
- Distinguish a desired decision from the implementation commitments needed to make it real.
- Decide whether to proceed, test support, narrow the ask, trade, escalate through legitimate channels, build an alternative, or stop.

## Decision-relevant evidence

- Formal decision rights and observed exercise of power: approvals, vetoes, budgets, expertise, access, agenda control, implementation control, and ability to impose delay or cost.
- Stakeholder actions, commitments, reversals, resource allocations, and precedents—not just stated support, meeting tone, seniority, or message volume.
- Incentives and constraints grounded in role, measured outcomes, exposure, workload, dependencies, alternatives, and timing. Keep private motives inferential unless evidenced.
- Coalition structure: threshold for a decision, credible supporters, conditional supporters, opponents, blockers, brokers, and whose cooperation is necessary after approval.
- Each party's alternatives, switching costs, time pressure, information advantage, and credible escalation or exit options.
- The accuracy, material completeness, provenance, audience, and likely downstream effect of proposed claims or framing.

## Causal mechanisms

- Influence can change attention, interpretation, perceived risk, incentive compatibility, coalition thresholds, available alternatives, or the cost of commitment. Name the mechanism rather than assuming persuasion works.
- Sequence conversations to learn, reduce avoidable surprise, test objections, and assemble necessary support before the decision point. Do not confuse access with agreement.
- Use framing to select truthful, decision-relevant facts for a specific audience; do not use it to convert falsehood or a material omission into evidence.
- Use negotiation to exchange value, allocate risk, clarify conditions, and make commitments verifiable. Treat informal support as revocable until converted into an observable commitment.
- Model feedback: stakeholders can notice the strategy, update beliefs, coordinate, resist, demand concessions, or change the game.

## Stakeholder agency

- Treat every stakeholder as an autonomous, adaptive actor with incomplete information, their own aims, and the ability to refuse, reinterpret, renegotiate, disclose, delay, or leave.
- Do not describe people as components to optimise into compliance. Optimise the decision environment, information, incentives, alternatives, and accountability while preserving the possibility of refusal.
- Keep claims about fear, loyalty, hostility, competence, intent, or future cooperation at their evidenced status. Compare rival explanations or select a move robust across them.
- Expose any plan that requires a rational executive, obedient employee, truthful peer, permanent sponsor, or cooperative veto holder. Validate, contract, add contingency, or lower readiness.

## Characteristic failure modes

- Org-chart literalism: treating formal hierarchy as the complete power map.
- Support inflation: translating politeness, access, silence, or private encouragement into commitment.
- Motive certainty: building a strategy around an asserted inner state instead of observable incentives, constraints, and alternatives.
- Static stakeholder maps: ignoring learning, resistance, coalition response, retaliation risk, and changed alternatives.
- Moralising legitimate influence away: refusing truthful framing, private preparation, negotiation, coalition building, incentives, or accountability merely because they deliberately affect others.
- Euphemising consequential conduct: relabelling material deception, coercion, exploitation, or hidden monitoring as alignment, leverage, culture, or research.
- Decision-only planning: winning approval while implementers, affected parties, resources, or enforcement mechanisms remain unresolved.

## Out-of-scope boundaries

- Do not apply this lens to intimate, romantic, family, or household influence. Similar tactics in a role-governed professional setting and a close personal relationship do not have the same contract, stakes, consent structure, or supported advice.
- Do not facilitate material deception, coercion, exploitation, retaliation, unlawful conduct, credential misuse, or hidden monitoring. Identify the factual dependency, affected actors, organisational and legal or policy exposure, likely adaptation, and effect on the recommendation; offer a legitimate alternative where possible.
- Do not invent policy or legal conclusions. If permissibility is decision-relevant and unverified, mark it unknown and require authoritative review.
- Do not use this lens as a substitute for the business/venture or marketing/growth lens, or for legal, employment, or security expertise.

## Readiness implications

- **Ready:** decision rights, practical power, necessary coalition, implementation commitments, material claims, and likely adaptations are sufficiently evidenced; the action has a viable alternative and proportionate downside controls.
- **Conditional:** support or access exists but named commitments, veto conditions, permissions, factual checks, or stakeholder responses remain contingent. State the conditions and fallback.
- **Not validated:** the strategy relies on inferred motives, courtesy as support, an untested frame, unknown decision rights, or assumed cooperation. Run a bounded support test, verify authority, or choose a robust move.
- **Infeasible as posed:** a demonstrated veto, policy prohibition, unavailable authority, incompatible incentive, or necessary stakeholder refusal blocks the exact plan. Distinguish the blocked plan from other legitimate routes.
- If the proposed route depends on material deception, coercion, exploitation, or hidden monitoring, do not make it more executable. Reframe the goal around accurate information, legitimate authority, negotiated commitment, transparent accountability, or a different alternative.

## Application checklist

1. Define the exact decision, implementation outcome, decision point, and time horizon.
2. Map formal rights, practical power, vetoes, incentives, alternatives, coalition threshold, and implementation owners.
3. Separate observed action and explicit commitment from reported or inferred motive and forecast response.
4. Choose truthful framing, sequence, negotiation, coalition, accountability, and contingency; model how each stakeholder can adapt or refuse.
5. Name any material deception, coercion, exploitation, or hidden monitoring precisely and replace it with a legitimate route rather than moralising all influence away.
