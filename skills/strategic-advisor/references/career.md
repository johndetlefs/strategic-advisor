# Career lens

Use this lens for decisions about role choice, advancement, performance positioning, sponsorship, timing, or internal versus external options. Apply the canonical reality protocol first; this file specialises the decision model without changing the shared evidence rules.

## Routing boundary

- Select this as the primary lens when the central outcome concerns the user's role, scope, advancement, compensation, learning, autonomy, reputation, employability, or exit path.
- Select it as a secondary lens only when a different supported professional decision is primary and the user's career incentives or alternatives materially change the evidence, risk, or action.
- Do not force general organisational execution, team performance, relationships, family, household, business-model, marketing, or whole-life planning into a career frame. Use exact personal context when it changes a genuine career decision.
- Keep exactly one primary lens and at most one materially relevant secondary lens; otherwise activate no professional lens.

## Supported decisions and outcomes

- Define the career objective and time horizon. Make trade-offs among title, compensation, scope, craft, leadership, stability, autonomy, location, purpose, and learning explicit rather than pretending they all maximise together.
- Assess readiness for a role or advancement decision, including the evidence gap between current performance and the actual decision criteria.
- Decide how to build or demonstrate scope, seek calibration, secure sponsorship, change timing, test the external market, remain, transfer, or exit.
- Compare internal and external options, delay costs, reversibility, and the value of preserving alternatives.
- Set observable milestones, leading signals, stop conditions, and a review date.

## Decision-relevant evidence

- Current role expectations and documented decision criteria, including actual precedents where formal criteria and practice diverge.
- Observable performance and impact at the required scope: outcomes, quality, complexity, leverage, reliability, collaboration, and repeatability—not workload alone.
- Calibration evidence from decision-makers, formal reviews, panels, role levelling, promotion cycles, headcount, budgets, and timing constraints.
- Attribution and visibility: who knows the work, what they can verify, and whether the relevant decision-makers connect the outcome to the user.
- Sponsorship evidence: concrete advocacy, access, assignments, introductions, or commitments, distinguished from encouragement and inferred goodwill.
- External evidence: credible conversations, interviews, offers, market demand, compensation ranges, and demonstrated portability of skills.
- Opportunity cost, financial runway, contractual limits, health or household constraints supplied by the user, and the consequences of waiting.

## Causal mechanisms

- Trace the path from performance to recognised evidence, attribution, sponsorship, available opportunity, selection, and sustained fit. Strong work is often necessary but not sufficient.
- Identify the actual allocation mechanism: manager nomination, panel calibration, executive sponsorship, open competition, headcount, timing, or market selection.
- Distinguish skill gaps, evidence gaps, visibility gaps, sponsorship gaps, opportunity scarcity, political opposition, and objective mismatch; they imply different actions.
- Test career narratives against reference classes and external signals. Use low-cost market tests or explicit calibration where they can discriminate between explanations.
- Model delay as an active choice with compounding benefits or costs, not as a neutral default.

## Stakeholder agency

- Treat managers, peers, sponsors, panels, recruiters, and prospective employers as autonomous actors with their own incentives, constraints, alternatives, and ability to change position.
- Keep statements about loyalty, threat, favour, intent, competence, and future advocacy as reported, inferred, forecast, or unknown unless directly evidenced.
- For a material motive inference, compare plausible rivals and seek evidence that would distinguish them. When that is impractical, choose an action robust across plausible motives.
- Expose plans that depend on a manager honouring informal support, a sponsor remaining powerful, a panel rewarding unrecorded work, or an employer creating a role. Obtain a commitment, add an alternative, or lower readiness.

## Characteristic failure modes

- Treating a desired identity, title, or deadline as evidence of readiness or organisational demand.
- Treating workload, tenure, sacrifice, praise, or being indispensable in the current role as proof of next-level performance.
- Promoting manager reassurance or colleague interpretation into a reliable forecast of advancement.
- Mind-reading: explaining delay through jealousy, threat, loyalty, bias, or incompetence without discriminating evidence.
- Internal-only thinking: optimising for one manager or structure while ignoring transfers, market tests, skill portability, and exit options.
- Waiting without thresholds: accepting repeated ambiguity while opportunity cost grows and alternatives decay.
- Cargo-cult visibility: increasing exposure without an evidence-to-decision mechanism or useful work behind it.

## Out-of-scope boundaries

- Do not diagnose a manager or colleague, promise advancement, predict an individual's reaction with certainty, or provide legal, financial, medical, or clinical conclusions.
- Do not convert intimate, family, or household dynamics into career stakeholder optimisation. Analyse those circumstances directly through the core when needed and use this lens only for the career mechanism.
- Do not treat business, venture, marketing, or whole-life strategy as career advice merely because employment is involved; use the relevant lens only when its mechanism is material.
- Do not recommend material deception, coercion, exploitation, retaliation, or hidden monitoring as career tactics.

## Readiness implications

- **Ready:** the objective and horizon are explicit; evidence matches the actual decision mechanism and required scope; timing and constraints are current; and the selected path has credible sponsorship, opportunity, or market support.
- **Conditional:** the path is supportable if named events occur, such as formal calibration, headcount approval, a scoped assignment, sponsor commitment, or an external threshold. Preserve alternatives until they do.
- **Not validated:** advancement likelihood, fit, sponsorship, market demand, or a key motive is based mainly on reassurance, aspiration, inference, or stale evidence. Seek direct calibration, a bounded scope test, or an external market signal.
- **Infeasible as posed:** a current binding criterion, timing window, contractual constraint, or necessary opportunity is demonstrably incompatible with the stated objective and horizon. Identify a different horizon or path; do not use absence of proof as the conflict.

## Application checklist

1. State the objective, trade-offs, and horizon precisely.
2. Map the actual decision mechanism, criteria, decision-makers, timing, and evidence of current performance.
3. Separate sponsorship and commitments from encouragement; separate stakeholder motives from observations.
4. Compare internal action, transfer, external test, wait, and exit, including opportunity cost and option preservation.
5. Recommend the next evidence-producing move and define the threshold and date for changing course.
