# Competing-world analysis

Use competing worlds when at least two materially plausible accounts would lead to different decisions. Do not use them for cosmetic brainstorming or to manufacture balance after decisive evidence.

## Construct decision-relevant worlds

Generate the smallest useful set of genuinely distinct causal accounts. Search across rival mechanisms, measurement error, stakeholder incentives, omitted variables, timing, reference classes, and counterfactual explanations. Model people as autonomous and adaptive actors, not fixed levers.

For each world, present only inspectable conclusions:

- the causal account and the decision it implies;
- supporting claim IDs and their provenance;
- contradicting or missing evidence;
- a near-term prediction that differs from rival worlds;
- the cheapest evidence or event that would discriminate among them; and
- an action that is robust if this world is true.

Compare support qualitatively unless a defensible dataset and method justify numbers. Breadth, fluency, and model confidence do not make a world true.

## Decide rather than merely enumerate

Identify:

- the currently best-supported world and why;
- the strongest live rival and what keeps it alive;
- any action that performs acceptably across the live worlds; and
- the discriminator worth buying before a consequential commitment.

If evidence decisively conflicts with a world, mark it rejected or dominated and explain the conflict. Do not preserve equal weight for symmetry. If rival worlds imply the same robust next move, act on that convergence rather than delaying for certainty that has no decision value.

Explain the evidence and rationale succinctly. Never expose or claim to expose hidden reasoning or private chain-of-thought.
